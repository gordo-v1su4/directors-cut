## Casting read

Kai arrives looking effortlessly cool and forgets that casting needs to see his eyes. The awkward correction should puncture the swagger without humiliating him. His quick apology, physical recovery, and funny scene line reveal why people forgive him and follow him even when the plan is questionable.

## FINAL SEEDANCE AUDITION PROMPT

Use @Image 1 as the definitive identity and starting costume lock for Kai “Hoodie” Santana. Preserve his exact face, apparent age 19, lean athletic build, platinum curls, warm brown skin, silver futuristic sunglasses, gray hoodie, early-2000s wardrobe, jewelry, and realistic natural texture. The glasses must not vanish, morph, duplicate, or reappear.

Create a 15-second cinematic casting-room audition in one continuous close-medium take. Kai is the only person visible. The casting reader is a mature British man with a calm, dry voice positioned beside the lens and completely outside the frame. Kai’s eye line stays naturally just beside the camera toward the reader. Plain quiet audition room, soft neutral light, locked eye-level camera with slight handheld realism, shallow depth of field, realistic pores, clean midtones, restrained film grain.

0–3s: Begin with Kai in a clean side profile wearing the sunglasses, loose and confidently ready. He turns fully front toward the reader’s just-off-lens eye line with a small movie-star smile. The unseen British reader says clearly and dryly: “Excuse me, can you take off the shades?”

3–7s: Kai freezes, realizes the mistake, and answers with a startled, completely natural stumble: “Oh—oh, shit. Sorry.” He laughs once under his breath, removes the sunglasses with one hand, nearly puts them in the wrong pocket, corrects himself, and places them fully out of frame. His apology is sheepish and charming, never performed like a punchline.

7–13s: Kai takes one breath, settles, and lets the embarrassment transform into the scene. With his bare eyes fully visible, he looks toward the unseen scene partner and says exactly once, starting cautiously and then winning himself back through amused confidence: “Okay. In my defense, that plan sounded much better before everybody followed me.”

13–15s: The reader gives him nothing. Kai holds the scene, then one corner of his mouth rises as he realizes the line is also true of this audition. End on a tiny hopeful nod and bright, exposed eyes—funny, capable, and immediately likable. No further dialogue.
