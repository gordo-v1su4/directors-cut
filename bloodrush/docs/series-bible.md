# Blood Rush — Series Bible

## Format and positioning

- Format: one-hour serialized supernatural college drama
- Setting: fall 2003, freshman year at a well-funded private Catholic college on the coastal California corridor
- Genre: supernatural drama, mystery, coming-of-age, dry comedy
- Audience: premium network or streaming young-adult/adult audience
- Visual promise: ordinary collegiate realism by day; Neon Gothic danger by night

## Logline

In fall 2003, cocky former football star Kai "Hoodie" Santana keeps a hidden circle of young vampires passing as ordinary college freshmen. When human freshman Mara Voss is turned without authorization and begins crying black blood, Kai must find who broke the group's oldest rule before her rare transformation exposes them all.

## Series engine

Every episode forces the group to balance recognizable freshman pressures—classes, money, sex, status, roommates, parties, work, family, and academic failure—with hunger, secrecy, loyalty, and the consequences of immortality. The supernatural plot does not replace college life; it makes every ordinary problem more dangerous.

Kai is the main lead and social center. He solves problems through charm, nerve, improvisation, and strategic lying. His methods work until they create a larger problem. Mara is the audience-entry co-lead: her unauthorized turning reveals the rules at the same time that her black tears prove the rules are incomplete.

Kai is gay and closeted. His sexuality is a human truth, not a symptom of vampirism, a flamboyant performance code, or a late-series trick. In the fall-2003 Catholic-college and family context, he has learned to let people assume he is straight because correcting them feels like risking every structure that currently keeps him safe. The series should reveal this through choices, intimacy, avoidance, and the rare person around whom his performance relaxes—not through stereotype.

The season mystery asks three questions:

1. Who turned Mara without permission?
2. Why does her body manifest black blood-tears?
3. What does her bloodline anomaly mean for the people already controlling campus vampire life?

## Tone

The emotional architecture is supernatural yearning and dangerous intimacy, but the series is not a romance retread. Relationships matter because trust is expensive when everyone is hiding something.

Humor is dry, situational, and character-specific:

- Kai improvises a confident answer before he understands the problem.
- Elias tries to assert older-brother authority while sharing freshman status with Rowan.
- Rowan corrects people at exactly the wrong emotional moment.
- Vampire logistics collide with 2003 technology, limited communication, and campus bureaucracy.
- Characters occasionally break tension with a small laugh, apology, or look—not a polished punchline.

Avoid quip chains, self-aware genre parody, conspicuous goth-vampire presentation, and nonstop despair.

Avoid treating Kai's sexuality as proof of deception, a predatory secret, comic embarrassment, or a one-to-one metaphor for vampirism. His closeted life can rhyme with the group's culture of passing while remaining morally and emotionally distinct from it.

## The 2003 rule

Everything must feel native to 2003:

- flip phones, landlines, voicemail, pay phones, AIM, burned CDs, portable CD players, early iPods, disposable cameras, CRT displays, campus computer labs
- VHS and DVD coexistence at Video Haven
- paper schedules, physical campus maps, printed flyers, message boards, and handwritten notes
- period cars, signage, product packaging, dorm technology, hair, makeup, and silhouettes
- no smartphones, social media feeds, streaming culture, LED strip décor, or modern influencer language

## The college rule

The campus is a maintained, well-funded private Catholic college, not a public high school, neglected state campus, or elite resort. Its visual identity combines coastal California Spanish Colonial Revival and restrained collegiate Catholic architecture with clean late-1990s renovations. Pale stucco, limestone trim, shaded arcades, clay tile, dark wood, mature palms and cypress, discreet crucifixes, and a chapel axis establish the institution without turning every frame into religious spectacle.

The buildings may show age, but not neglect: no graffiti, broken mirrors, water-damaged ceilings, filthy grout, vandalized fixtures, nicotine-stained walls, or abandoned-building decay. Avoid high-school lockers, homeroom language, juvenile posters, trophy cases, and undersized classroom furniture. Uniforms are not required during the current audition and visual-development pass.

## Vampire rules

### Daylight and passing

Sunlight does not destroy these vampires. Daylight suppresses their strongest traits and helps them pass, but it also leaves them slightly cold, pale, overstimulated, or emotionally flat. Most people read them as unusually intense, sleep-deprived, strange, or beautiful—not visibly supernatural.

Normal scenes use human-looking eyes, restrained complexions, and ordinary period wardrobe. Gold eyes, fangs, black tears, and predatory movement appear only when hunger, fear, rage, feeding, or transformation motivates them.

### Hunger

Hunger sharpens hearing, scent, reflexes, aggression, and fixation. Early symptoms are behavioral: staring too long, tracking a pulse, missing conversational rhythm, clenching the jaw, or losing the thread of a cover story.

### The amber vial

The ornate amber vial contains a suppressant that lowers hunger and helps young vampires pass. It is not a cure. Frequent use dulls emotion and memory, and scarcity gives whoever controls the supply leverage over the group.

### Turning

Turning another person without group approval is forbidden. A new vampire is unstable, traceable, and capable of exposing everyone. Mara's turning is an unauthorized act, not an accident the group can quietly ignore.

### Exposure protocol

When a human witnesses something impossible, the group escalates in this order:

1. Deny and normalize what happened.
2. Discredit the witness or create a mundane explanation.
3. Intimidate or isolate the witness.
4. Bring the problem to Kai.

Turning or killing a witness without approval is a serious violation. The threat that "something has to happen" remains real, but the moral argument over what that means creates drama.

### Mara's black tears

The tears are black, blood-like fluid produced during a rare transformation anomaly. They are not ordinary vampire makeup and should not appear on other characters. At first, nobody knows whether they indicate power, illness, ancestry, or contamination. Their ultimate significance is a dormant bloodline that established vampires believed no longer existed.

## Visual language

### Day

Natural campus light, institutional fluorescents, muted early-2000s color, lived-in dorms, ordinary wardrobes, and faces that feel accessible. The supernatural enters through behavior, framing, reflections, or a detail held one beat too long.

### Night

Bruised magenta, clinical blue, sodium amber, wet asphalt, fluorescent glare, glass reflections, CRT flicker, and neon practicals. Neon Gothic is a lighting and danger system—not a costume mandate.

### Camera

- performance-first medium close-ups for dialogue
- controlled handheld realism for social pressure
- macro detail only for motivated eyes, fangs, blood, the amber vial, or period objects
- fast cutting reserved for hunger, pursuit, parties, or a loss of control
- no generic trailer montage language inside audition scenes

## Core locations

- **Video Haven:** Kai's workplace, social base, and practical cover; owned by his human maternal uncle Rafael "Rafa" Santana; trapdoor in the storage room leads to the campus tunnel network; full bible in `docs/locations/video-haven/`
- **campus bathroom:** Mara's transformation chamber and recurring mirror motif
- **college quad / walkway:** public normalcy by day, predatory openness by night
- **freshman classroom:** the pressure to behave normally while senses and hunger intrude
- **dorm / off-campus party house:** recruitment, temptation, status, and loss of control
- **The Owl's Nest:** late-night diner — black coffee, neon signs, coven meetings away from campus eyes
- **Sigma Phi house basement:** pledge and transformation site; where turns succeed or fail
- **The Underneath:** cavern network beneath Veridian; bone gallery and amber source tree at its heart
- **Registrar's Office:** Mr. Yoshida and Yuki's institutional cover; hallway proximity, never speaking
