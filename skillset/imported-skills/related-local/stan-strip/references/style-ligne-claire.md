# Style anchor — ligne claire (Tintin / Hergé tradition)

The clean-line European tradition — Tintin, Asterix, Blake et Mortimer, Yoko Tsuno. Restrained, balanced, immediately readable, with uniform line weight and flat colour. The opposite of Marvel/DC's bold dramatic inks.

## The anchor paragraph (paste into every page prompt)

> Ligne claire European comic-book style in the Hergé / Tintin tradition. Uniform-weight clean black ink lines (no varying line weight, no inking flourishes) defining all forms with calm precision. Flat solid colours, no halftone, no gradients — areas of pure colour bounded crisply by the line work. Slightly muted, harmonious palette favouring earth tones, sage greens, dusty blues, warm reds, ochre yellows, cream backgrounds (#F2EBD7). Thin black panel borders (1.5px) with clean white gutters. Compositions are balanced, often symmetrical, with deep clear backgrounds rendered in the same uniform line weight as foreground subjects (no atmospheric blur, everything in focus). Faces are simplified, expressive through clear gesture and brow rather than detailed rendering. Speech bubbles are clean rounded ovals with thin black borders, dialogue in a clean readable lettering (less hand-feel than American comics, more typeset feel). Caption boxes are thin yellow or cream rectangles. Sound effects are restrained — small, integrated, lower-case-friendly, never breaking the panel borders. Overall feel: calm, balanced, beautifully composed, classical European comics craft. No watermarks, no real brand logos, no halftone dots, no dramatic ink shadows, no Ben-Day shading, no kinetic motion-line excess.

## When to use

- Adventure stories with restraint
- European-inflected work (Belgian, French, Italian)
- Children's comics where bold-ink violence would feel wrong
- Any work valuing clarity, calm, and craftsmanship over kinetic energy
- Period pieces (the style itself reads as mid-20th-century)

## When NOT to use

- Action/superhero work needing dramatic ink — use classic American
- Manga/anime adaptations — use manga
- Anything wanting hand-feel or textural irregularity — use indie zine

## Key differences from classic American

- **No varying line weight** — every line is the same thickness
- **No halftone** — colour areas are completely flat
- **No SFX flourish** — sound effects exist but stay restrained, never break panels
- **Calmer compositions** — fewer Dutch tilts, less foreshortening, more symmetry
- **More background detail** — every part of the panel is rendered with equal care, no foreground/background hierarchy through line weight
