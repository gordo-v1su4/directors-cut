# Style anchor — indie / zine

The hand-drawn, photocopier-feel, limited-palette tradition of indie comics, mini-comics, and zines (Adrian Tomine, Jillian Tamaki, Kate Beaton, Sarah Glidden, Anders Nilsen, Lynda Barry tradition). Loose ink line, deliberate imperfection, restrained palette, the visible feel of a hand making it.

## The anchor paragraph (paste into every page prompt)

> Indie zine / mini-comic art style with a hand-drawn, slightly imperfect quality. Loose confident black ink line with visible variation in pressure and small honest imperfections (no sterile precision). Limited palette — typically black ink plus one or two flat spot colours (rust red, mustard yellow, sage green, dusty pink, or muted blue) applied in flat washes, never gradients. Off-white slightly-warm paper background tone (#F5EFE2) inside panels, with a very subtle paper grain texture. Thin black panel borders drawn by hand (slightly uneven, not ruler-perfect, ~2px equivalent). White gutters between panels. Faces are simplified, expressive, character-first — gesture and silhouette carry the emotion rather than detailed rendering. Backgrounds are sparse, often just enough to suggest place. Speech bubbles are hand-drawn ovals with slightly irregular outlines, dialogue in casual hand-lettered-feel mixed-case lettering. Caption boxes are simple thin-bordered rectangles with the same hand-lettered text. Sound effects are restrained, integrated into the line work, often lower-case, never breaking out of the panels. Slight photocopier-feel artefacts — a tiny bit of edge softness, an occasional thin line break — are welcome. Overall feel: handmade, intimate, character-driven, the visible texture of a real zine made by a real person on a Tuesday afternoon. No watermarks, no real brand logos, no slick digital gradients, no Marvel-style ink flourish, no halftone dots.

## Default colour-to-role mapping

| Element | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Soft black | #2A2422 | Loose ink line (softer than pure black, like a real pen) |
| Warm cream | #F5EFE2 | Paper background, panel interior |
| Spot rust | #B5413A | Single accent option |
| Spot mustard | #D9A82A | Single accent option |
| Spot sage | #8FAA86 | Single accent option |
| Spot dusty pink | #D8A8B8 | Single accent option |
| Spot muted blue | #6B89A8 | Single accent option |

Pick ONE spot colour for the project (occasionally two if they harmonise) and use it consistently — that restraint is the style.

## When to use

- Slice-of-life stories
- Memoir / autobiographical comics
- Character studies and quiet drama
- Comedy that works on awkwardness rather than gags
- Anything where the *handmade feel* is part of the meaning
- Self-published / personal-project work where the zine register signals authenticity

## When NOT to use

- Action / superhero comics — the loose line undersells the kinetic energy
- Anything where slick polish is part of the brand
- Children's books expecting bright saturated colour
- Manga/anime adaptations

## Why the looseness matters

GPT Image 2's natural tendency is toward clean precision. To get genuine zine-feel, the anchor specifically calls out:
- Variation in line pressure
- Slight imperfections being welcome
- Hand-drawn (not ruler-drawn) panel borders
- Photocopier artefacts being part of the look
- Lower-case integrated SFX rather than dramatic display lettering

These instructions deliberately work against the model's polish reflex. If the output comes back too clean, strengthen those phrases.
