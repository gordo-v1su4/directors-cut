# Directorial modes — Classical vs Deconstructive Wuxia

This is the single most important reference in the skill. Every storyboard the skill produces commits to one of these modes (or a deliberate hinge between them). The mode determines camera support, framing, clarity, choreography legibility, atmospheric content, editing rhythm, weapon treatment, and the moral universe of the clip.

Read this fully before drafting either prompt. Mode-undecided prompts produce mode-averaged boards, and mode-averaged boards produce generic martial-arts collages that look like neither King Hu nor Tsui Hark.

## The two poles

| Axis | Classical (King Hu) | Deconstructive (Tsui Hark *The Blade*) |
|---|---|---|
| **Camera ideology** | Proscenium witness — the camera attends a performance | Vérité documentary inside the fantasy — the camera smuggled itself into the jianghu |
| **Camera support** | Tripod, dolly, low- and high-angle crane | Handheld shoulder, long lens (200mm+), monopod |
| **Clarity** | Total — silhouette always readable, every move legible | Withheld — violence outside frame, obscured, or too close to resolve |
| **Framing** | Symmetrical, balanced, architecture-exploited (doorways, columns, gateways) | Off-axis, peeking through foreground objects, foreground occlusion welcomed |
| **Movement motivation** | Dialogue, blocking, action beat — every move has a named cause | Curiosity, distraction, late reaction — the operator finds the action a beat late |
| **Cutting grammar** | Wide → medium → close, orderly progression, cuts at phrase punctuation | Double-cuts, withholdings, sudden lateral shifts, refusal to release on the climax |
| **Mistakes** | Hidden to preserve fantasy | Embraced — operator reframes, out-of-focus passages, visible zooms kept in |
| **Frame content** | Clean — beautiful sets, immaculate costumes, controlled atmosphere | Filled with weather and matter — wind, rain, fire, dust, mud, hanging cloth, crowds |
| **Choreography legibility** | Hero's silhouette readable against background; full body in frame during signature moves | Combat glimpsed, partial, smeared — reconstructed from sound and aftermath |
| **Texas switch (actor/stuntman)** | Hidden behind clean cuts and matched action | Run as visible three-beat: actor → stuntman → actor again, seams part of the rhythm |
| **Code of chivalry** | Honoured — combatants face each other, salute, ceremonial structure | Violated — combat without preamble, dishonoured deaths, killing as function not ritual |
| **Hero archetype** | Noble, mastered, supported by friends and teachers, prevails on screen | Flawed, evasive, isolated, fights for survival not honour, often does not prevail in scene |
| **Editorial style** | Holds long enough to see each phrase as a phrase; no stuttering | Holds past the expected cut, double-cuts at scene seams, refuses the money shot |
| **Production surface** | Display case — fantasy must not be punctured | Vérité — punctures the fantasy as a structural choice |
| **Weapon framing** | Sword as artefact — close, ceremonial, calligraphic in frame | Sword as functional tool — smeared, dirtied, partially seen |
| **Audio register** | Long silences punctuated by single percussion strikes; orchestral score with traditional instruments | Continuous weather audio fills the bed; score interrupted, fragmented, or absent |
| **Moral universe** | Ordered cosmos governed by the chivalric code | Pure violence, survivalist, no higher ideal organising the world |
| **Outcome of the scene** | Resolution, button, victor stands | Aftermath, ambiguity, exit walked rather than triumph posed |

## Choosing a mode

The source material almost always tells you which mode is right. Use these flags:

**Classical flags** (any one of these and classical is the default):
- Tone words: *graceful*, *elegant*, *ceremonial*, *honoured*, *legendary*, *mythic*, *poetic*
- Structural beats: a salute, a face-off, a master-disciple bond, a vow, a victor's stance, a sworn oath
- Setting cues: temple steps at dawn, calligraphy on a wall, a tea house, a courtyard, a bamboo forest at noon (filtered light, vertical structure used for graceful aerial choreography)
- Hero posture: noble, supported by friends or teachers, fights for honour or duty, prevails
- Source material register: drawn from Jin Yong, Wuxia Pian classics, *Crouching Tiger Hidden Dragon*, *House of Flying Daggers* in its composed register

**Deconstructive flags** (any one of these and deconstructive is the default):
- Tone words: *brutal*, *muddy*, *vérité*, *raw*, *survivalist*, *broken*, *desperate*, *grim*, *fragmented*
- Structural beats: an ambush, a coward, a master who runs, a duel without face-off, a dishonoured death, a survival exit instead of triumph
- Setting cues: marketplace at dusk, a derelict shrine, a riverside reedbed in storm, a remote inn at night, mud, fire, blood
- Hero posture: flawed, isolated, missing a limb or skill or text, fights for survival, does not prevail in the scene
- Source material register: drawn from Tsui Hark *The Blade*, Gu Long's grimmer work, fight scenes in *Ashes of Time*

**Hinge flags** (the source material starts in one register and breaks into the other):
- A daydreamed duel that hard-cuts to the witnessed reality
- A ceremonial face-off that ends with a dishonourable strike
- A master entering classically who dies dishonourably
- An audience-surrogate narrator (like Ling in *The Blade*) whose romanticised vision is shattered by what they see
- A scene that opens in clean classical light and progressively fills with deconstructive weather as the moral universe collapses

When the user explicitly names a mode, use it. When they name a director, infer: King Hu / early Tsui Hark / Yuen Woo-ping = classical; Tsui Hark *The Blade* / *Ashes of Time*-era Wong Kar-wai action = deconstructive.

## Mode-specific prompt phrasings

### Classical phrasings to use verbatim in storyboard captions

For camera and movement:
- `static tripod, both combatants balanced in frame`
- `slow dolly-in motivated by [character]'s step forward`
- `parallel tracking matching the combatant's path along the colonnade`
- `low-angle crane up at the moment of the leap, silhouette against sky`
- `high-angle crane down on the salute, both combatants symmetrical in the courtyard`

For framing and choreography:
- `silhouette readable against [background] — full body in frame`
- `architecture exploited: framed through the doorway, both columns visible`
- `the choreographic phrase reads as a complete movement, no cut inside it`
- `cut at the natural punctuation between phrases`

For atmosphere and surface:
- `clean filtered light through bamboo, controlled atmospheric haze`
- `immaculate robes, controlled wind in fabric`
- `golden-hour key light on the master, soft fill on the disciple`

For combat ritual:
- `combatants face each other, weapons addressed but not yet drawn`
- `salute exchanged, robes settle`
- `the strike registers cleanly, the blade trajectory legible`

### Deconstructive phrasings to use verbatim in storyboard captions

For camera and movement:
- `handheld, long-lens (200mm), peeking through foreground bamboo`
- `late reframe — operator finds the action a beat after it has begun`
- `hesitant zoom in, focus pull arrives late`
- `lateral attention shift — camera looks away to a witness mid-strike`
- `tilt to weather (rain sheet, dust raked by light) mid-action`

For withheld clarity:
- `the strike happens just outside frame; only the body falling into shot is visible`
- `combat within frame but obscured by foreground figure / hanging cloth / wall`
- `weapon so close to lens it cannot resolve — a blur, a flash, a smear`
- `the act reconstructed from sound and aftermath, not seen directly`

For atmosphere and surface (these belong in nearly every frame):
- `wind visible in cloth, dust kicked up by the strike`
- `rain in sheets across the frame, blade glints through it`
- `foreground fire smoke trails across the action`
- `mud splash on the body, the ground dark and wet`
- `hanging laundry / crowds / animals breaking up the sightline`

For broken combat ritual:
- `combat begins without preamble; no face-off, no salute`
- `[honoured figure] killed without dignity`
- `killing is functional, not ceremonial — pure violence`
- `survival exit, urgent — the hero walking away, frame held empty after`

## Hinge sequences — naming the break

A hinge is the most specific kind of mode commitment. It demands you name **which classical signature is being inverted at which exact shot**.

Useful hinge structures (each describes a different inversion):

1. **Classical setup, deconstructive payoff** — stage the entrance, the face-off, the chivalric beat in classical grammar; then have the act itself happen wrong (off-frame, dishonourably, without the expected cut). The break is at the strike: shots 1-3 classical, shots 4-8 deconstructive. The board labels shot 4 as `MODE BREAK: face-off resolves dishonourably`.

2. **Surrogate fantasy, witnessed reality** — shot 1 is a daydream (clean classical mode, idealised duel, romanticised palette). Shot 2 hard-cuts to handheld vérité for the actual event. The break is at shot 2; the board labels it `MODE BREAK: daydream → witnessed reality`.

3. **Mode drift across the sequence** — open classical, drift toward deconstruction as the moral universe collapses around the hero. No single cut is the break; instead, atmosphere progressively fills across the eight frames (shot 1 clean, shot 4 some weather, shot 8 frame full of mud/fire/wind). The board labels this as `MODE DRIFT: classical → deconstructive across the sequence`.

4. **Single-shot mode break** — within one shot, begin composed and motivated, then let the operator visibly lose composure (a late reframe, a sudden zoom) at the moment the world tips. The board labels that single shot with both modes and a `MODE BREAK MID-SHOT` flag.

For any hinge, the storyboard's mode declaration in the header reads:

```
DIRECTORIAL MODE: HINGE — opens classical, breaks at shot [N] ([reason for the break])
```

And the affected shot's caption block carries a small `MODE BREAK` chip.

## Why mode commitment matters for the Seedance handoff

Seedance 2 reads the storyboard image as visual reference for camera language, lighting, character look, and pacing. A mode-decided board gives Seedance a coherent set of cues:

- Classical board → Seedance inherits composed framing, silhouette legibility, motivated movement, clean atmosphere
- Deconstructive board → Seedance inherits handheld energy, withheld clarity, atmospheric load, late reframes

A mode-undecided board is internally contradictory — composed framing on some frames, vérité smear on others, no clear atmospheric register. Seedance averages the cues and produces a clip that reads as neither mode. The clip looks "fine" but it does not look *like wuxia*. Avoid this by committing.

## Common mode mistakes

**Asking the same shot for both silhouette discipline and withheld clarity.** These are mutually exclusive within a single frame. Pick one per shot. Hinge sequences alternate cleanly on a per-shot basis, never within a frame.

**Treating atmosphere as decoration in deconstructive mode.** In deconstructive mode atmosphere is structural — it must be named in nearly every frame's caption. Treating it as decoration (mentioned only in the mood panel) produces a board that looks classical with weather added, not deconstructive.

**Honouring the chivalric code in a deconstructive prompt.** If the user has flagged deconstructive, do not stage a classical face-off, salute, or ceremonial structure. The code is supposed to be broken; staging it intact is a mode contradiction.

**Withholding clarity in a classical prompt.** Classical mode promises legibility. A storyboard frame that hides the strike outside frame in classical mode breaks the genre's pleasure. If the user wants withheld clarity, the mode is deconstructive.

**Vague mode declaration.** "Wuxia, kind of like Tsui Hark" is not a mode commitment. Either Classical or Deconstructive (or a named hinge). Force the choice in the prompt.
