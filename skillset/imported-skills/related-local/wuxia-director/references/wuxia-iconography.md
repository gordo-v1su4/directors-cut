# Wuxia iconography — recurring motifs treated for both modes

This reference covers the recurring visual motifs of the wuxia register, with mode-aware prompt-ready phrasings for each. Use these when writing the storyboard's set photo, environment description, and per-frame action descriptions.

The same motif reads very differently in classical vs deconstructive mode. The motif itself is genre-shared; the *treatment* commits to the mode.

## The blade itself

The blade is the visual representative of both the genre tradition and (in deconstructive mode) the instrument that breaks that tradition.

**Classical treatment:**
- Frame the weapon as artefact: close, ceremonial, calligraphic in the cell
- Set photo of the weapon: hero-shot on a silk cloth, single softbox, jade or wooden background, weapon pristine
- Per-frame: "[combatant] (white robes, jian with jade pommel)"
- Decisive moment: weapon trajectory legible, gold path-of-blade arc, sustained steel ring SFX

**Deconstructive treatment:**
- Frame the weapon as functional tool: smeared, dirtied, partially seen
- Set photo of the weapon: blade resting in mud, blood-flecked, leather wrap fraying, single low-key practical light from off-frame
- Per-frame: "[combatant] (mud-spattered dao, leather wrap dark with blood)"
- Decisive moment: weapon obscured by foreground or so close to lens it smears; blood-red path-of-blade arc rather than gold; SFX is wet impact, not steel ring

**Common weapons and their wuxia register:**

| Weapon | Type | Classical register | Deconstructive register |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Jian* | Straight double-edged sword | Master's weapon, scholar-warrior, jade pommel | Master's weapon stripped of nobility, plain hilt, dark blade |
| *Dao* | Single-edged curved sabre | Soldier's weapon, sworn brother, leather wrap | Bandit's weapon, mud-caked, leather worn black with blood |
| Spear | Long polearm | Cavalry weapon, formation discipline, red tassel | Footsoldier's weapon, broken haft, no tassel |
| Staff | Wooden quarterstaff | Monk's weapon, discipline, simple | Survivor's weapon, splintered, pragmatic |
| Paired blades | Twin shortswords | Dancer-warrior, paired flow | Cornered survivor's last resort |
| Whip | Chain whip / rope dart | Showy weapon, performance discipline | Trap weapon, ambush, struck from concealment |
| Flying-claw | Hook on a chain | Climber's tool, infiltration | Ambush weapon, struck from above |
| Three-section staff | Linked staves | Versatile master's weapon | Practical weapon, broken-and-rebound |

## The bamboo forest

The most iconic wuxia location. Vertical structure, filtered light, acoustic privacy.

**Classical treatment (bamboo forest at noon or filtered afternoon):**
- Filtered light through canopy in clean shafts
- Vertical structure used for graceful aerial choreography (combatants leap up the bamboo, balance on tips, descend)
- Camera positioned to keep silhouettes clean against bright canopy
- Atmospheric haze controlled, not loaded
- Per-frame: "shafts of light filtered through canopy", "combatant balanced on bamboo tip, silhouette clean against filtered sky"

**Deconstructive treatment (bamboo forest at dusk or in storm):**
- Foreground bamboo cuts the sightline; camera peeks through it
- Wind in canopy is visible and audible; bamboo creak structural
- Lower-level combat in mud at the base, no aerial work
- Per-frame: "long-lens through foreground bamboo", "wind in canopy", "mud at the bamboo roots", "bamboo creak"

## The temple (steps, gate, shrine)

Ceremonial wuxia space. Stone, incense, monks, oaths.

**Classical treatment (temple at dawn or noon):**
- Stone steps used for vertical staging — combatants on different levels, master above, disciple below
- Symmetrical architecture (gate, columns, dragon-tile roof) for composition
- Filtered incense haze, controlled
- Salute, vow, ceremonial face-off appropriate
- Per-frame: "framed through the temple gate", "both combatants symmetrical on the stone steps"

**Deconstructive treatment (derelict shrine at night or in rain):**
- Shrine in disrepair — broken roof tiles, weeds, rain through gaps
- Single practical light source (lantern, lightning flash, dying fire)
- No symmetrical staging — combatants positioned off-axis
- The shrine's spiritual significance is being violated — code-of-chivalry break appropriate
- Per-frame: "rain through the broken roof", "single lantern, dying", "the shrine's altar overturned"

## The marketplace (or town square)

Public arena where heroes are tested.

**Classical treatment (marketplace at noon, lively):**
- Stalls with goods (silk, peaches, fish, cloth) clearly visible
- Crowd watching the confrontation, organised
- The stage for the intervention beat — a hero arriving to right a wrong
- Composed wide establishing, both combatants visible
- Per-frame: "stalls of silk and peaches", "crowd parts to reveal the confrontation"

**Deconstructive treatment (marketplace at dusk, ambush, code violated):**
- Stalls partially packed up; goods scattered; chaotic
- Crowd as foreground occlusion — bodies cutting the sightline, no organised audience
- The place where the chivalric code is publicly violated (a Shaolin monk killed, a master struck from behind)
- Long-lens through the crowd, handheld weave between bodies
- Per-frame: "ambush through the dispersing crowd", "stalls overturned, goods in mud"

## The remote inn

Chamber for character and consequence — the wuxia equivalent of a saloon.

**Classical treatment (remote inn, lantern-lit evening, organised):**
- Composed long takes around tables, candle and lantern light
- Wide shots of the main room; characters at tables, server moving between
- Conversation-driven scenes, oaths sworn, disguises maintained
- Per-frame: "wide of the inn's main room", "lantern light, controlled warmth"

**Deconstructive treatment (remote inn at night, fight in the corners):**
- Tight handheld in the corners and back rooms
- Single lantern as practical, dim and flickering
- Tables overturned, an ambush from above (a beam, a balcony)
- Per-frame: "handheld through the back room", "lantern flicker, single source", "tables overturned, lantern oil pooling"

## The bamboo fortress / mountain canyon / distant peak

The mythical geography of the jianghu — where the master lives, where the secret manual is hidden, where the final duel happens.

**Classical treatment (mountain canyon at sunrise, distant peak with cloud):**
- Wide vista shot, scale established
- Combatants small in frame, framed against the landscape
- Soft mist, golden hour, controlled atmosphere
- Per-frame: "wide vista of the canyon at sunrise", "combatants small against the cliff face"

**Deconstructive treatment (mountain canyon in storm, jagged unforgiving):**
- Stark high-contrast cliff against grey sky
- Combatants exposed, weather punishing
- No wide vista beauty shot; mid-shots only, weather closes in
- Per-frame: "rain on the cliff face", "wind so loud the combatants must shout"

## The training courtyard

The place of the master-disciple bond.

**Classical treatment:**
- Sand or stone floor, sword stand, scroll on a low table
- Master in white robes, disciple in blue training robe
- Choreography that reads as instruction — the disciple imitating the master's form
- Per-frame: "training courtyard at dawn", "sword stand visible", "master demonstrates the form"

**Deconstructive treatment:**
- Training courtyard abandoned or violated — sword stand toppled, scroll torn
- Disciple alone, working from an incomplete manual (pages missing)
- The master figure absent or hostile
- Per-frame: "abandoned training courtyard", "incomplete manual on the floor", "disciple alone, working through the form imperfectly"

## The bridge / waterfall / reedbed

Liminal jianghu spaces — places of ambush, places of farewell, places of decision.

**Classical treatment:**
- Wooden footbridge over still water, reflection clean
- Waterfall as backdrop for a duel, water spray controlled and beautiful
- Reedbed at dawn, mist controlled
- Per-frame: "wooden bridge over the still pond", "waterfall as backdrop, controlled spray"

**Deconstructive treatment:**
- Bridge in disrepair, water below muddy and fast
- Waterfall as a noise wall and visual obstruction; ambush within the spray
- Reedbed in storm, reeds whipped flat, mud knee-deep
- Per-frame: "broken bridge over fast muddy water", "waterfall obscures the strike"

## The decisive prop (the manual, the talisman, the tally)

Wuxia stories often turn on a specific object: the secret manual that teaches the technique, the talisman that proves identity, the tally that records grievances.

**Classical treatment:**
- The object framed as artefact in an insert shot
- Calligraphic detail visible, surface clean
- Decisive emotional beat registers on the holder's face
- Per-frame: "insert close on the manual, calligraphy legible"

**Deconstructive treatment:**
- The object damaged, partial, fragmentary
- Pages missing, talisman cracked, tally illegible
- The hero working from incomplete information; failure baked in
- Per-frame: "the manual, half its pages missing, mud on the cover"

## How to use this reference

When writing the storyboard prompt:

1. Identify which jianghu location the user has implied
2. Look up the location's classical and deconstructive treatments above
3. Pick the treatment matching the chosen mode
4. Use the prompt-ready phrasings (in italic-quotes above) verbatim or adapted in the relevant frame's action description
5. Repeat for any other motifs in the scene (the weapon, a decisive prop, the master)

This is what makes a wuxia board read as wuxia rather than as generic martial arts. The motifs are genre-defining; the mode-aware treatment is what makes the board commit.
