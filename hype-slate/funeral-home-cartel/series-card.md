# FUNERAL HOME CARTEL

## Logline

When a debt-drowned daughter inherits her family’s Midwestern funeral home, she finds the embalming room, floral shop, and crematorium have been moving money, product, and sometimes people — and her talent for comforting the grieving makes her the operation’s perfect new face.

## Tagline

Grief is good cover.

## Pertains to brief

- **Ozark DNA, different skin:** family business, moral math, institutions as camouflage — but death rituals instead of lake houses
- **Prestige drama:** embalming aesthetics, florals, hearses, visitation etiquette, Catholic/Protestant/cultural rites
- **Vice grit without street cliché:** logistics through a place society refuses to look at closely
- **Shonda:** you think inheritance melodrama → the “pre-need packages” are the books of a pipeline
- **Breaking Bad:** the soft-skilled sibling (arrangements, people, paperwork) becomes the criminal CEO because death already taught her composure

## What this is NOT

- Campy Addams-Family crime comedy (unless a future tonal variant is requested)
- Narcos with a hearse sticker
- Pure horror / corpse exploitation gore show
- A gimmick that forgets family and guilt

## What this IS

A grounded family prestige thriller where funeral infrastructure — bodies, ash, flowers, hearses, “out of town” transfers — hides distribution and laundering.  
Visually opposite of typical cartel stories: hush, lacquer, lilies, fluorescent prep rooms, black suits in daylight.

## Pilot engine

**She is not in the cartel yet.**

**Ruth Anne Cole** comes home for her father’s funeral, planning to sell the home and escape.  
She discovers the books are “fine” in a way small businesses never are — and a body in the cooler isn’t the person on the death certificate.  
Staff (longtime embalmer, florist aunt, driver) treat her like a child who shouldn’t open doors.  
Pilot pressure: a shipment / cremation window can’t miss, or people die who aren’t already dead.  
Pilot end: Ruth signs one “transfer authorization” to keep the staff safe for 48 hours — and her signature is now in the machine.

## Characters

- **Ruth Anne Cole** — prodigal daughter; arrangement specialist; composure as inheritance
- **Uncle Pete** — embalmer; old-school hands; knows every sin in the basement
- **Aunt Mari** — florist / front-of-house saint; laundering through “event design”
- **Diego** — hearse / transfer driver; the road is the real border
- **Detective Han** — death investigator who smells wrong paperwork; not a cartoon hero

## World

Family funeral home in a corridor city with real logistics access (think Midwest → river/highway → eventual Miami/port adjacency via “out of state” partners).  
Visuals: visitation parlors, rose coolers, prep-room steel, formaldehyde light, hearses at dawn, crematorium heat shimmer, prayer cards, laminated menus of packages, church parking lots, casserole grief.  
Subculture: funeral directors’ associations, florists, coroners, clergy, “cash families,” immigrant deathcare networks.

## Shonda hook

Ruth delivers a perfect eulogy arrangement for a beloved teacher — town hugs her as the new face of the home.  
Floor drop: the cremation she authorized “for the family” was the disposal window for a product load; the teacher’s urn is clean… the second urn in the same run is not on any certificate.

## Breaking Bad pivot

Ruth’s gift is **people under pressure** — widows, cops, priests, vendors.  
She doesn’t cook product; she makes everyone comfortable enough not to look.  
She starts rewriting SOPs to reduce exposure (cleaner books, better death certificates, fewer panic cremations) — and becomes the most dangerous operator the network has ever had: the one who understands grief as UX.

## Season engine

Family succession thriller + logistics thriller:

1. Inherit the home / survive the first missed window  
2. Sibling or cousin war over “going legitimate”  
3. Expand via cemetery contracts and multi-state transfer licenses  
4. A public mass-casualty event floods the home with real bodies — cover and crisis at once  
5. Federal deathcare audit as season boss  

Episode rule: every episode opens with a real death that matters… and a false one hiding inside it.

## Teaser rules

**Part 1 may show:** visitation hush, florals, hearse chrome, prep room gloss, Ruth comforting a widow, one wrong toe tag / second urn / ledger that balances too well.

**Part 2 may show:** night transfer, crematorium load, cash in flower boxes, Ruth’s signature, a detective at the back door — still prestige, still quiet, then one hard sting.
