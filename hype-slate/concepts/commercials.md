# Commercials (C01–C05)

Brand-agnostic concepts. Each is a 15–30s culture spot that can later lock to a real client (drink, telco, fashion, streamer, auto).

---

## C01 — DROP THE ROOM

**Format:** 30s brand film  
**Tone:** Chaotic joy, club-core

**Logline:** A dead college lecture snaps into a full rave the second someone cracks a can — and the professor was the headliner the whole time.

**Short description:**
- Directly rides the Charli xcx / Poppi “vibes class” energy that cut through Super Bowl culture chatter.
- Product is the mode-switch, not a speech.
- Horse, flamethrower optional; attitude mandatory.

**Teaser hook:** Chalk screech → beat drop → “It’s a vibes thing.”

**Why now:** Super Bowl LX showed culture > hard sell; classroom-to-club is instantly memeable and trailerable.

**Best brand fit:** soft drink / energy / fashion / headphones

---

## C02 — I WANT IT THAT WAY 2.0

**Format:** 60s surprise-concert spot  
**Tone:** Nostalgia shock, warm chaos

**Logline:** Real customers in a flagship store think they’re waiting for a phone upgrade — until a 90s/00s duo walks out of the stockroom and plays the song that raised them.

**Short description:**
- T-Mobile / Backstreet Boys model: real reactions, live mini-concert, Times-Square-scale spillout.
- Camera stays handheld and late-night cold so it feels stolen, not staged.
- End card is secondary to the scream.

**Teaser hook:** “Please take a number—” — bassline. Recognition. Screaming.

**Why now:** Nostalgia duo tours are top trending concert searches; surprise-concert ads still outperform product demos.

**Best brand fit:** telco / credit card / streaming / ticketing

## C03 — SHAKE THE BOTS

**Format:** 30s AI-irony spot  
**Tone:** Glossy absurd, self-aware

**Logline:** Two party robots try to be human for one night — they nail the drip, fail the drink, and accidentally invent a better toast than anyone in the club.

**Short description:**
- Leans into Super Bowl AI-ad chatter without being a tech pitch.
- Joke is robots wanting what humans waste.
- Product is the thing that makes the night feel real.

**Teaser hook:** Robot tries to sip. Spill. “We’re still learning.” Cut to humans doing worse.

**Why now:** AI was everywhere in 2026 ads; the winners will be irony + spectacle, not feature lists.

**Best brand fit:** vodka / RTD / dating app / gaming peripheral

---

## C04 — HALFTIME IN YOUR HEADPHONES

**Format:** 45–60s streamer / audio brand film  
**Tone:** Stadium intimacy

**Logline:** A loner puts on headphones on a bus — and the world around them becomes their private Super Bowl halftime, choreographed to one song, ending on their stop.

**Short description:**
- Bad Bunny–scale live spectacle compressed into personal audio fantasy.
- Bus → street → skyline choreography; nobody else hears it.
- Brand promise: the biggest stage fits in your ears.

**Teaser hook:** Bus fluorescent lights become spotlights. Commuters become dancers. Doors open on a stadium.

**Why now:** Halftime crushed ads on engagement in 2026; personalizing that power is the commercial opportunity.

**Best brand fit:** music streamer / headphones / phone / sportsbook (careful)

---

## C05 — BUTTER YELLOW RUN

**Format:** 30s fashion / color film  
**Tone:** Kinetic, sun-drenched, obsessive

**Logline:** A single butter-yellow object is stolen, chased, traded, and crowned across a city — and every person who touches it changes outfit, status, and fate.

**Short description:**
- Google Summergeist “butter yellow” trend as pure cinema.
- No dialogue; color as character.
- Ends on a product that isn’t explained — just inevitable.

**Teaser hook:** Yellow bag. Yellow taxi. Yellow dress. Yellow finish line.

**Why now:** Trend colors dominate search; fashion films that feel like chase scenes outperform lookbooks.

**Best brand fit:** fashion house / sneaker / beauty / auto exterior colorway
